# Constrained ground-station multihoming

> Superseded by `../gs_address_policy/`. In this sweep transit satellites could still minimise
> over all K of a station's attachments, so its routing numbers are an optimistic bound, not one
> fixed address per packet. The radio-assignment counts still stand.

Focused evaluation of A-prime attachment addressing with a small number K of simultaneous
ground-station attachments.

- Code: LEOPath `591510e` (`feat/gs-multihoming-sweep`).
- Server output: `~/leopath-k-sweep-591510e` on `6genablers-dlt-3`.
- Completion: 336/336 jobs, 112 experiment cells, no failed jobs.
- Matrix: Telesat, OneWeb, Kuiper and Starlink; no failure and 5% random ISL loss; K=1, 2 and
  4; independent top-K and exclusive assignments; matched link-state and topological
  variants plus visibility baselines.
- Physical constraint: a ground station may receive up to K attachments, but a satellite may
  be assigned to at most one evaluated ground station. The independent policy is retained as
  an unconstrained upper bound rather than as the proposed hardware model.

The summary files in this directory are generated by:

```bash
python -m leopath.experiments.summarize_failure_sweep \
  --input ~/leopath-k-sweep-591510e \
  --output-dir ~/leopath-k-sweep-591510e/summary
```

`failure_sweep_seeds.csv` contains one row per run, `failure_sweep_summary.csv` aggregates
the seeds, and `failure_sweep_tables.md` renders the comparison. The server's raw run
directories are not copied into this repository.

## Main checks

- K=2 exclusive assigned all 48 requested links (24 ground stations times two) in every
  snapshot on all four shells.
- K=4 exclusive assigned all 96 requested links on OneWeb, Kuiper and Starlink. Telesat
  averaged 93.05, because the satellite-side capacity constraint binds in its smaller shell.
- With no injected failures, no delivered topological walk changed the egress selected at the
  source, for any K or constellation.
- With 5% random ISL loss, K=2 changed egress in transit on 0.49% (Telesat), 3.17% (OneWeb),
  1.68% (Kuiper) and 0.34% (Starlink) of delivered pairs. The current set-valued model is
  therefore not strict fixed-address A-prime under failures.

The last result is a semantic boundary, not merely another metric. In strict A-prime the
source chooses one advertised attachment address and puts that one destination in the packet;
transit satellites route toward it and cannot silently choose another member of the K set.
Changing to another address must be an explicit source or flow failover. The focused sweep
quantifies K and the radio constraint, but the final attachment-addressing evaluation still
needs that packet-level pinning.
